## 04. Featured work

Ordered by strength of product argument, not chronology. Commercial work first, concept work last.

**01 · Parfee** — On-demand household services, Nice, France · *with a PM*
*Multi-role admin system, 4 connected interfaces*
The strongest case. Cascading booking states (Paid / Not Confirmed / Booked-but-Unpaid) across four roles is genuine systems thinking; scheduling that supports both simultaneous and sequential task execution is a real modelling decision. Leads the grid.

**02 · Fiducia** — SaaS automation for Skool.com communities, Germany · *with a PM and developers*
The decision to foreground: you matched Skool.com's own UI patterns so the tool reads as native rather than bolt-on. That is a product tradeoff — you gave up a distinct visual identity to buy user familiarity. Say it out loud; it is exactly the reasoning a design lead looks for. Also the one project with a stated scope figure (80 hours) and a full redesign.

**03 · TextYourTenant** — Bulk SMS for property managers, USA
Carried by the client quote about engineering handoff. Angle it as the *design system and handoff* case.

**04 · Travellite** — Mobile app, tour search · **`Concept project`**
Academic case study from the mobile design course. **Label it explicitly as a concept project.**

> **Why it stays, and why it stays labelled.** It is your only native mobile case — drop it and the "Mobile" claim in the proof bar has nothing behind it. But a hiring manager identifies student work in seconds: no client, no constraints, no compromises. If they spot it *after* assuming it was commercial, it retroactively damages trust in the other three. Labelling it costs nothing and buys credibility. It also earns something the client work cannot show: how you reason when nobody is constraining you.

### Case study template

Replaces the skeleton's `Problem → Process → Decisions → Outcome`.

```
Title        Project name + one line on what the product is
Meta bar     Client · Country · Year · Role · Team · Duration · Platforms

01 Context   What the product is, who uses it, what the business needed
02 Problem   What was broken, and how you knew it was broken
03 Decisions 2–3 forks. For each: options considered → what you chose → what you gave up
04 Outcome   Scope shipped · client reaction · what you would change now

Quote        Client feedback, where it exists
```

**Three deliberate changes from the skeleton:**

1. **`Process` is cut.** It reliably becomes a generic double-diamond retelling. Low signal.
2. **`Context` is added, and it carries your role and team.** The most common question about a freelance portfolio is *"what did he do, and what did someone else do?"* Answer it before it is asked.
3. **`Outcome` is redefined** — see below.

`Decisions` does the actual work. A decision without a sacrifice is not a decision: every fork needs the thing you gave up.

### Outcome without metrics

These were MVPs; product metrics do not exist. Do not invent them, and do not leave the section empty. Three substitutes, all honest:

- **Scope numbers** — you *do* have these: 4 role interfaces, 3 booking states, mobile + desktop, 80 hours, a design system with tokens/states/variants. Concrete and verifiable.
- **Client reaction** — the testimonials are the outcome evidence for a project with no dashboard.
- **What I would change now** — the strongest lever you have. A middle designer lists what they built; a senior tells you what was wrong with it. With no metrics available, this section is where seniority actually shows. Write it for all four cases and do not soften it.

## Technical decisions

**Case studies must be linkable.** The drawer has no URL, so a hiring manager cannot forward a specific case to a colleague — which is exactly what they do. Minimum fix: hash routing (`/#/work/parfee`) so the drawer is deep-linkable. Better: the 4 featured cases become real pages, the 3 secondary ones stay lightweight.

**Marquee** stays as visual rhythm but carries no argument. "UX Research · Product Strategy · IA" is what everyone writes; do not let it occupy the position where evidence belongs.
